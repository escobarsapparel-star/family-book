Children, photos & privacy

Child & Family Media Rules

Effective 19 September 2026 · Version 2026-09-19-v1 · South Africa
Family Book is built for family Memories, including photos of children. That makes children’s privacy a core rule, not a small-print issue.

1. Who is a child?

For these rules, a child means a person under 18. South African law gives children special protection and requires their best interests to be treated as an important consideration.

2. Permission before uploading

Only upload a child’s photo, video, name, birthday, school information, location or other identifying information when you are legally entitled to do so. Where POPIA requires it, obtain consent from a parent, guardian or other competent person before processing or sharing the child’s personal information.

Being an aunt, uncle, sibling, cousin, grandparent or other relative does not automatically mean you have legal authority to publish or share another person’s child’s information.

3. No blanket consent for every child

Accepting Family Book’s legal terms confirms your responsibility for the content you personally upload or share. It does not create blanket consent for every child in the family and does not override a parent, guardian, competent person, court order or applicable law.

4. Respect the child’s dignity and best interests

Do not post content that is exploitative, sexualised, humiliating, abusive, degrading or likely to cause serious embarrassment, bullying, safety risk or reputational harm to a child. Where appropriate for the child’s age and maturity, respect the child’s own wishes about a photo or story being shared.

5. Avoid unnecessary sensitive details

Think carefully before sharing information that could expose a child’s routine or identity outside the family. Avoid unnecessary publication of school addresses, live location, identity/passport details, medical information, private contact details or other information that could create a safety or privacy risk.

6. Family-only visibility still requires care

Family Book is designed for private family groups. However, authorised relatives may still be able to screenshot, download, export or re-share content. Uploaders must consider that practical risk before posting child-related media.

7. Tagging children

Tagging a child links the child’s identity to a Memory. Only tag a child when you are entitled to identify that child in the family group. If a parent, guardian, competent person or the child (where appropriate) asks for a tag or image to be removed, the request should be taken seriously and reviewed promptly.

8. Removal requests

A parent, guardian, competent person or affected person may ask the Family Book Administrator to review or remove child-related content. Family Book may remove or restrict content where there is a credible privacy, safety, consent or legal concern, even if the original uploader disagrees.

9. Exporting and sharing outside Family Book

Family-tree exports, downloaded media and content shared through WhatsApp, social media, email or another service leave Family Book’s private environment. The person who exports or re-shares that content is responsible for having the necessary authority and for the privacy consequences of that external sharing.

10. Legal basis

POPIA specifically regulates the processing of children’s personal information, and the Information Regulator publishes guidance on this topic. The Information Regulator guidance, POPIA, Children’s Act 38 of 2005, and the Constitution are important reference points.
If there is a dispute about who may consent for a particular child, do not upload or re-share the content until the issue is resolved. Family Book cannot decide parental-rights disputes or replace legal advice.
