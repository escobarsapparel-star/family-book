Access to information

PAIA Access Notice

Effective 19 September 2026 · Version 2026-09-19-v1 · South Africa
The Promotion of Access to Information Act 2 of 2000 (“PAIA”) gives effect to the constitutional right of access to information in certain circumstances. This notice explains the practical Family Book access process while the service remains a private Beta.

1. Status of this notice

Family Book is presently operated as a private Beta family service. This page is an access-to-information notice and is not represented as a complete section 51 PAIA manual. If Family Book’s final legal or business structure requires a formal section 51 manual, the responsible party should complete and publish that manual using the Information Regulator’s current prescribed guidance and include the formal Information Officer and service-address details.

2. Records Family Book may hold

  • Account and authentication records.
  • Family membership, invitation and access records.
  • Family profiles, relationships and family-tree information.
  • Optional contact details and privacy settings.
  • Memories, photos, videos, albums, tags and related metadata.
  • Family Wall posts, comments and reactions.
  • Calendar events, birthdays, anniversaries and reminders.
  • Notification preferences, in-app notifications and registered push-device records.
  • Legal-acceptance and security/audit records.

3. Requests for your own personal information

A user may ask whether Family Book holds personal information about them and may request access, correction or deletion where POPIA permits. Family Book may require reasonable proof of identity before disclosing or changing records.

4. Requests for records concerning other people

Access to a record about another family member is not automatic. Family Book must consider privacy, confidentiality, children’s rights and any lawful ground for refusing or limiting access. A requester may need to show that the record is required for the exercise or protection of a right where PAIA requires that showing.

5. How to make a request

Submit the request to the Family Book Administrator who manages or provided your access. Clearly identify the record requested, the requester, the reason for the request where relevant, and the preferred method of receiving the response. Identity verification may be requested before private family records are disclosed.

6. Prescribed forms, fees and procedure

Where a formal PAIA request is required, the prescribed PAIA forms, statutory time periods and fees may apply. The Information Regulator publishes the current PAIA Guide, request forms and complaint procedures on its official website.

7. Complaints and external guidance

A requester who is dissatisfied with the handling of a formal PAIA matter may have rights to complain to the Information Regulator or pursue another remedy provided by law. Users should consult the current Information Regulator guidance for the applicable process and time limits.

8. Information Regulator

Official PAIA guidance, manuals and forms are available from the Information Regulator (South Africa). The Promotion of Access to Information Act 2 of 2000 is available from the South African Government.

9. Relationship with POPIA

Requests for a person’s own personal information may also fall under POPIA. Family Book’s handling of personal information is described in the Privacy & POPIA Notice.

Before a public Play Store or commercial launch, the Family Book operator should confirm whether a formal PAIA section 51 manual is required for the final legal structure, add the responsible party/Information Officer details and have the legal pack reviewed by a South African privacy practitioner.
