Privacy & POPIA

Privacy Notice

Effective 19 September 2026 · Version 2026-09-19-v1 · South Africa
Family Book is a private family-sharing service. This notice explains how personal information is handled. It is designed around the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (“POPIA”), but it is not a substitute for advice from a South African privacy lawyer or Information Officer.

1. Who this notice applies to

This notice applies to Family Book users, invited family members, people represented in a family tree, and people appearing in photos, videos, Memories, posts, calendar items or other family content.

2. Responsible party and privacy contact

Family Book is currently operated as a private Beta service in South Africa. The operational privacy contact is the Family Book Administrator. Privacy, access, correction, deletion and removal requests should be raised with the Family Book Administrator who manages the service. Formal Information Officer and service-address details should be added before a public commercial launch.

3. Information Family Book may process

  • Account information such as name, email address, authentication identifiers and profile photo.
  • Family information such as relationships, birthdays, family-tree links, biography information and optional contact details.
  • Family content such as photos, videos, Memories, captions, tags, comments, reactions, Family Wall posts and albums.
  • Calendar information such as family events, anniversaries, birthdays, locations and event notes.
  • Notification information such as notification preferences and device push-notification tokens.
  • Optional location information only where a user deliberately adds a place or check-in.
  • Technical and security information needed to operate the service, including session, device, service logs and legal-acceptance records.

4. Why information is processed

Family Book processes information to authenticate users, connect relatives to a private family group, display the family tree, store and share family Memories, operate the Family Wall and calendar, send requested notifications, protect accounts, provide support, investigate misuse or security incidents, and meet legal obligations.

Depending on the situation, processing may rely on one or more grounds permitted by POPIA, including consent where required, performance of an agreement, compliance with law, protection of a legitimate interest of the data subject, or legitimate interests of the responsible party or a third party.

5. Who can see family content

Family Book is designed for private family groups rather than public publishing. Content can be visible to members of the same family group according to the app’s privacy and access controls. Users should not assume that another family member cannot save, screenshot, download or re-share content outside Family Book.

6. Children’s personal information

Photos, names, birthdays, family relationships and other information about a person under 18 can be children’s personal information. POPIA places special restrictions on processing children’s personal information. Users must only upload or share a child’s image or identifying information when they are legally entitled to do so and, where required, have authorisation from a parent, guardian or other competent person. The child’s best interests and privacy should be respected.

Family Book does not treat a general click on “Accept” as permission for every child in a family. The user who uploads or shares child-related content remains responsible for having the appropriate authority for that content.

7. Service providers and international processing

Family Book uses technical service providers to operate the service, including authentication/database hosting, cloud media storage, static web hosting and push-notification delivery. The current Supabase database is hosted in the EU Central region. Other providers may process or store information outside South Africa. Where international transfers occur, Family Book will take reasonable steps to use appropriate safeguards and protections consistent with POPIA, including the requirements applicable to cross-border transfers.

8. Security

Family Book uses technical and organisational safeguards intended to protect personal information, including authenticated access, database access controls, private media handling and account-based permissions. No online service can promise absolute security. If there are reasonable grounds to believe personal information has been accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person, the responsible party must handle the incident in accordance with POPIA’s security-compromise requirements, including notification where required.

9. Retention and deletion

Information is kept for as long as reasonably needed to provide Family Book, maintain family history requested by users, protect the service, resolve disputes and comply with applicable law. Users may request correction or deletion of personal information where POPIA allows it. Some records may need to be retained for legal, security, audit or backup purposes for a limited period.

10. Your privacy rights

Subject to POPIA and any lawful limitations, a data subject may request confirmation of whether information is held, request access, request correction or deletion, object to certain processing, withdraw consent where processing depends on consent, and lodge a complaint with the Information Regulator.

11. Browser/app storage and third-party links

Family Book uses essential browser or app storage for sessions, settings and functionality. Family Book does not currently operate an advertising-cookie programme. If a user opens a third-party link or service, such as a video or external website, that third party’s own privacy terms may apply.

12. Complaints

Users should first raise privacy concerns with the Family Book Administrator so the matter can be investigated. A person may also contact or complain to the Information Regulator (South Africa) using the Regulator’s official channels.

13. Legal framework

Key sources include the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013, the Information Regulator guidance, the Children’s Act 38 of 2005, and the Constitution of South Africa.
Before Family Book is offered broadly to the public, the operator should have the complete legal pack reviewed by a South African privacy practitioner, confirm the responsible party/Information Officer details, and complete any registrations, PAIA material or other compliance steps that apply to the final business structure.
